AI misinformation about your brand happens when an assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Perplexity states something false about your business, such as a wrong price, an old address or a feature you do not offer. You spot it by asking the assistants the questions your buyers ask and checking every answer against the facts. You fix it by correcting the public sources the assistants learn from and cite, then checking again.

The rest of this guide explains why these errors happen, how to find them in a structured way, and how to fix them without guessing.

## Why AI assistants get brands wrong

AI assistants do not look facts up in a single trusted database. They generate answers from a mix of what the model learned during training and, for many engines, pages they retrieve from the web at the moment you ask. Errors creep in at both stages.

- **Outdated training data.** A model learns from a snapshot of the web. If you changed your pricing, rebranded or closed a location after that snapshot, the model may still describe the old version.
- **Conflicting sources.** If your website says one thing and an old directory listing says another, the assistant may pick the wrong one or blend them.
- **Thin information.** When there is little written about a small brand, a model may fill gaps with plausible-sounding details. This is often called a hallucination.
- **Name confusion.** Brands that share a name with another company, product or place are commonly mixed up.
- **Misread pages.** A comparison page, a forum thread or a satirical article can be summarised as if it were a plain statement of fact.

Understanding which cause applies matters, because each one has a different fix.

## The kinds of errors to look for

Not every mistake carries the same risk. It helps to sort what you find into a few categories so you can prioritise.

| Error type | Example | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial facts | Wrong price, wrong plan names, a free tier that does not exist | High: sets false expectations before a sale |
| Availability | Says you serve a region you do not, or that a product is discontinued | High: sends buyers elsewhere |
| Identity | Confuses you with a similarly named company | High: mixes your reputation with theirs |
| Capability | Claims a feature or integration you do not have, or misses one you do | Medium to high |
| Contact and location | Old address, phone number or opening hours | Medium |
| Attribution | Credits your work or product to a competitor | Medium |
| Tone and framing | Describes you as a budget option when you position as premium | Lower, but shapes perception |

For example, a software company might find that one assistant says it has no mobile app, while another lists an integration it retired two years ago. Both are worth fixing, but the missing app is more likely to lose a buyer.

## How to spot AI misinformation about your brand

Finding errors is mostly a matter of asking the right questions consistently and recording the answers.

### Build a set of test prompts

Start with the questions real buyers ask. Mix direct brand questions with category questions where your brand might come up.

1. "What is [your brand] and what does it do?"
2. "How much does [your brand] cost?"
3. "Does [your brand] offer [key feature or service]?"
4. "Where is [your brand] located?" or "Does [your brand] serve [region]?"
5. "What are the alternatives to [your brand]?"
6. "What is the best [your category] for [a common use case]?"
7. "[Your brand] vs [main competitor]: which is better for [use case]?"

If you are not sure which questions your buyers use, ask your sales and support teams which questions they hear most often, and phrase the prompts the way customers do.

### Ask every major assistant

Each assistant has different training data and retrieval behaviour, so an error in one may not appear in another. Run the same prompts in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity. Use a fresh session for each prompt where possible, so earlier questions do not shape later answers.

### Check answers against a fact sheet

Write a short internal fact sheet before you start: official name, what you sell, current plans and prices, regions served, key features, founding facts you are happy to publish, and contact details. Mark each answer line by line as correct, wrong, outdated or missing.

### Record the sources

Many engines, Perplexity in particular, show the pages they used. Others will often tell you if you ask "What sources is this based on?" Note every cited URL next to each error. This is the most useful information you will collect, because it tells you where to make the fix.

## Trace each error to its source

Once you have a list of errors, work out where each one comes from. Ask three questions.

- **Is the wrong fact on a page I control?** Check your own site first, including old blog posts, help articles, PDFs and archived pricing pages that may still be public.
- **Is it on a third-party page?** Directories, review sites, marketplaces, partner pages, old press coverage and forum threads are common sources.
- **Is there no source at all?** If no page states the wrong fact, the model may be filling a gap. That usually means you need clearer, more prominent information, not a takedown.

Tracing takes some detective work, but it turns a vague worry ("AI says strange things about us") into a concrete to-do list.

## How to fix wrong AI answers

There is no single button that edits what a model believes. The reliable approach is to make the correct information easy to find, easy to read and consistent everywhere.

### Fix your own website first

Your site is the source you control fully, and it is often the first page an engine retrieves for a brand question.

- Update or remove outdated pages, and redirect old URLs to the current version.
- State key facts in plain sentences near the top of relevant pages: what you do, who it is for, where you operate and what it costs.
- Add an about page and a clear pricing page if you do not have them.
- Use structured data (for example, Organization and Product markup) so your name, logo, contact details and offerings are machine-readable.
- Make sure AI crawlers are not blocked from the pages that hold your key facts.

Our [AI-ready website checklist](/blog/ai-ready-website-checklist) walks through structured data and crawler access in detail.

### Correct third-party sources

- Claim and update your business profiles on major directories, maps and review platforms.
- Ask partners, resellers and marketplaces to update descriptions and pricing.
- Contact publishers of articles with factual errors and ask for a correction, giving the evidence.
- Answer outdated forum or community questions with a current, factual reply where the platform allows it.

### Fill the gaps

If assistants invent details because little is written about you, publish more clear, factual content. A detailed FAQ page, product documentation and plain comparison pages give engines something accurate to work from. Our guide to [writing content AI assistants can cite](/blog/content-ai-assistants-cite) explains how to structure those pages.

### Use the feedback tools

Most assistants let you rate an answer or report a problem. Use these when you find a clear error. Treat it as a useful signal to the provider, not as the main fix.

## Prioritise what to fix first

You will rarely have time to fix everything at once. A simple priority checklist helps:

1. Errors that affect buying decisions: price, availability, core features.
2. Identity confusion with another company.
3. Errors that appear in several assistants, which suggests a widely used source.
4. Errors tied to a cited source you can edit today.
5. Errors in tone or framing.

Fixing a single source that several assistants cite can clear up more answers than many small edits elsewhere.

## Monitor over time

AI answers change as models are updated and as engines recrawl the web. A fix that works this month can be undone by a new model version or by a fresh article that repeats the old fact.

- Re-run your test prompts on a regular schedule, such as monthly, and after any major change to your pricing or product.
- Keep a log of each error, the source you fixed and the date you fixed it.
- Watch for new errors after launches, rebrands or price changes, since these are the moments when old information is most likely to linger.

Tracking accuracy alongside visibility gives a fuller picture. Being mentioned often is not much help if the mentions are wrong, which is why it pays to [measure your share of voice in AI answers](/blog/measure-ai-share-of-voice) and check accuracy in the same review.

## Key takeaways

- AI assistants get brands wrong because of outdated training data, conflicting sources, thin information and name confusion.
- Find errors by asking all five major assistants the questions buyers ask and checking each answer against a written fact sheet.
- Record cited sources, because they show you where to make the fix.
- Fix your own website first, then correct third-party listings, then fill information gaps with clear factual content.
- Re-check regularly, especially after pricing, product or brand changes.
