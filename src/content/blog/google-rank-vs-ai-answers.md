Ranking on Google does not mean AI assistants will mention you, because the two systems produce different kinds of output from different inputs. Google ranks individual pages in a list, while assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity write a single answer that draws on training data, live search results and third-party descriptions of your brand, and often names only a few businesses.

A strong search position still helps. But it is one input among several, and many brands discover that the questions where they rank well are not always the questions where AI recommends them. This article explains why the gap appears and how to close it.

## Two different jobs: ranking pages versus writing answers

A search engine's core job is to find and order pages. When someone searches, they see many results and choose which to open. Your page can rank and still leave the reader to decide.

An AI assistant's job is to answer the question directly. It reads or recalls information, combines it, and writes a response. The output is a judgement, not a list of options. That difference changes what matters:

| Factor | What it means for a Google ranking | What it means for an AI mention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit of competition | Your page versus other pages | Your brand versus other brands |
| What wins | A page that matches the query and earns trust signals | A brand the assistant can describe confidently from several sources |
| How many get seen | Many results per page | Often a few names |
| Role of other sites | Mostly links and references pointing to you | What other sites actually say about you |
| Stability | Positions change gradually | Answers can vary between runs |

## Where AI answers come from

To understand the gap, it helps to know the two main ways an assistant gets its information. The exact methods differ by product and change often, so treat this as the general picture rather than a description of any one system.

### Training data

Language models are trained on large amounts of text gathered up to a cutoff date. What the model knows about your brand from that text shapes how it describes you when it answers from memory. If your business changed recently, or was rarely written about before the cutoff, the model may know little or hold outdated details.

### Live retrieval

Many assistants can search the web while answering and then summarize what they find, sometimes showing citations. Perplexity is well known for showing its sources, and ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok can also search the web in many situations. When retrieval is involved, current search results matter more, but the assistant still decides which pages to read, which facts to keep and which brands to name.

Google also shows AI-generated summaries above some search results. Those summaries sit next to the traditional rankings, which is a clear example of how the same query can produce both a ranked list and a written answer that mentions different businesses.

## Seven reasons you rank but are not mentioned

### 1. The assistant answered from memory

If the assistant did not search, your current ranking was never considered. What counts is how your brand was described in the text the model learned from.

### 2. Third-party sources describe your competitors more

Assistants commonly lean on review sites, directories, comparison articles, forums and news coverage. A competitor that appears in many "best of" lists may be named ahead of you even if your own page ranks higher.

### 3. The prompt is not the same as the keyword

People search with short phrases but ask assistants full questions, often with context: "I run a small bakery and need a booking tool that works on tablets." Your page may rank for "booking software" but never address the specific need in the prompt.

### 4. Your page is hard to summarize

A page can rank well because of its authority and links while still being vague. If it never states plainly who the product is for, what it costs or how it differs, an assistant has little it can confidently repeat.

### 5. Your brand is not clearly defined as an entity

If your business name is generic, shared with other companies, or described inconsistently across the web, an assistant may not connect the page that ranks to the brand it should name.

### 6. Crawlers are blocked

AI companies operate their own crawlers, and some websites block them in robots.txt, sometimes by accident. If an assistant's crawler cannot read your site, it has less of your own content to draw from.

### 7. Different search indexes

Not every assistant relies on Google. Some use other search providers or their own indexes, so your position in those systems may be different from what you see on Google.

## The four situations you will find

When you compare Google rankings with AI mentions for the same questions, each question usually falls into one of four groups.

| Situation | What it usually means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rank well and mentioned | Your content and reputation support each other | Protect it and keep facts current |
| Rank well, not mentioned | Search strength is not turning into AI mentions | Improve clarity and third-party coverage |
| Rank poorly, mentioned | Your reputation is carrying you | Improve the page that should rank |
| Rank poorly, not mentioned | Neither system sees you as a good answer | Decide if the question is worth pursuing |

The second group is usually the most valuable to work on first, because you already have pages with search strength to build on.

## How to diagnose the gap

Use this checklist for each question where you rank well but are not mentioned:

1. Open the page that ranks. Does it answer the exact question in its first few paragraphs?
2. Does it state who you serve, what you offer, where you operate and how you differ, in plain text?
3. Ask the assistant the question again and read the brands it names. Where do those brands appear online that you do not?
4. If the assistant shows sources, open them. Are you listed there? Is the information about you current?
5. Ask the assistant directly what it knows about your brand. Note any errors or gaps.
6. Check robots.txt with your developer to confirm you are not blocking crawlers you want to allow.
7. Search for your brand name and check that your name, description and category are consistent across major listings.

## What to fix first

Work from the changes that are quickest and most reliable:

- **Answer the question on the page that already ranks.** Add a direct, short answer near the top, then the detail.
- **State the facts plainly.** Products, pricing approach, audience, location and key differences should be easy to quote.
- **Close coverage gaps.** If the sources an assistant cites list your competitors but not you, look for genuine ways to be included, such as accurate directory listings, reviews from real customers or a correction request to an outdated article.
- **Make your brand unambiguous.** Use the same name and description everywhere, and explain on your about page what your business is.
- **Re-check after changes.** Assistants and search indexes update on their own timelines, so track results over several weeks rather than expecting an immediate change.

## Do not abandon SEO

None of this means traditional search optimization stops mattering. Pages that rank well are more likely to be found when an assistant searches the web, and good SEO practices such as clear structure, fast pages and helpful content support AI visibility too. The point is that SEO alone is not the full picture. You now need to watch both. If you are new to the idea, start with [what AI search visibility is and why it matters](/blog/ai-search-visibility-explained).

## Key takeaways

- Google ranks pages; AI assistants write answers that name brands.
- Assistants draw on training data, live retrieval and third-party sources, so ranking is only one input.
- Common causes of the gap include thin third-party coverage, vague pages, unclear brand identity, blocked crawlers and different search indexes.
- Compare your Google rank and AI mentions question by question to find the gaps worth fixing.
- Start with pages that already rank, make their answers direct, and build genuine coverage elsewhere.
