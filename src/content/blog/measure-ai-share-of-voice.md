To measure your brand's share of voice in AI answers, ask a fixed set of buyer questions in each AI assistant, record which brands are mentioned in each answer, and calculate the share of answers that include you compared with your competitors. Repeat the same checks on a regular schedule, and track placement and accuracy alongside the raw mention count.

That is the short version. The rest of this guide explains how to choose the prompts, how to score answers consistently and how to read the results without over-reacting to noise.

## What share of voice means in AI answers

In traditional marketing, share of voice describes how much of the conversation in a market is about you rather than your competitors. In search, it often means your share of visibility across a set of keywords.

In AI answers the idea is similar, but the unit is different. Instead of a page of links, you get a single written answer that names a few brands. Your share of voice is how often you are one of them.

This matters because many buyers now ask assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity for recommendations before they visit any website. If you are not in the answer, you may never make the shortlist. We explain the wider context in [what AI search visibility is and why it matters](/blog/ai-search-visibility-explained).

## Step 1: Choose prompts that reflect real buyer questions

Your measurement is only as good as the questions you ask. Prompts should sound like something a real buyer would type or say.

Cover several types of intent:

- **Category discovery**: "What are the best tools for managing restaurant reservations?"
- **Problem-led**: "How can a small restaurant reduce no-shows?"
- **Comparison**: "What are alternatives to [competitor]?"
- **Constraint-led**: "Which reservation tools work for a restaurant with one location and a small budget?"

Leave out questions that name your brand directly when you are measuring share of voice. Those are useful for checking accuracy, but they do not tell you whether an assistant would think of you unprompted.

For more on how buyers actually phrase these questions, see [how buyers ask AI assistants for recommendations](/blog/buyer-questions-ai-assistants).

## Step 2: Define your competitor set

Share of voice is relative, so decide in advance who you are measuring against. Include:

- The direct competitors you lose deals to
- The brands that assistants tend to name in your category, even if you do not consider them direct rivals
- Any large general-purpose providers that often appear in answers

Keep this list stable. If you add competitors halfway through, your share of voice will appear to drop even if nothing changed in the answers.

## Step 3: Record each answer in a consistent way

For every prompt and every assistant, capture the same fields. A simple spreadsheet works.

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Prompt | The exact wording used |
| Assistant | ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok or Perplexity |
| Date | When the check was run |
| Brands mentioned | Every brand named, in order of appearance |
| Your position | First, second, later, or not mentioned |
| Accuracy | Correct, partly wrong or wrong |
| Sources cited | Any links the assistant showed |
| Sentiment | Positive, neutral or negative description |

Use the same account settings each time where you can, and avoid running checks while signed in to an account with a long history of your own brand searches, which may influence the answers you see.

## Step 4: Calculate the core metrics

Once you have the records, a few simple metrics tell most of the story.

### Mention rate

The share of prompts where your brand appears at least once. For example, if you ran a set of eight prompts in one assistant and were mentioned in two of them, your mention rate for that assistant would be two out of eight.

### Share of voice

Your mentions divided by the total mentions of every brand in your competitor set, across the same answers. This shows how much of the available attention goes to you.

### Average position

Where you appear when you are mentioned. Being named first is usually more valuable than being listed last.

### Accuracy rate

The share of mentions where the assistant described you correctly. A high mention rate with poor accuracy is a warning sign, not a success. Our guide to [spotting and fixing AI mistakes about your brand](/blog/ai-misinformation-about-your-brand) covers what to do when descriptions are wrong.

### Citation share

For assistants that show sources, how often your own pages are among them. This helps you see which of your pages are being used.

## Step 5: Deal with answer variation

AI answers are generated, not looked up, so the same prompt can produce different results. This is normal and it means one check is not a measurement.

Ways to handle it:

1. **Repeat each prompt** more than once per check and record each run.
2. **Look at trends, not single points.** A change that persists across several checks is more meaningful than one surprising answer.
3. **Keep prompt wording fixed.** Small changes in wording can change the answer, so do not edit prompts between checks.
4. **Note model or product changes.** When an assistant releases an update, mark it in your records so you can explain shifts.

## Step 6: Compare AI share of voice with Google rankings

AI share of voice is most useful when you read it next to your search performance. The interesting cases are where the two disagree:

| Situation | What it often suggests |
| --- | --- |
| High Google rank, low AI mentions | Your page ranks, but assistants find clearer or more widely repeated descriptions of competitors |
| Low Google rank, high AI mentions | Third-party sources or training data describe you well, even if your pages are not ranking |
| High on both | A strong position worth protecting |
| Low on both | A foundational content and authority gap |

The first case is common and is usually the best place to start, because you already have a page with search visibility to build on.

## Step 7: Set a reporting rhythm

Decide how often you will re-run the checks and who will see the results. A practical approach:

- Run the full prompt set on a regular schedule, such as every few weeks.
- Report share of voice per assistant and overall.
- Highlight prompts where your position changed, up or down.
- List the actions taken since the last report, so you can link changes to causes.

Keep the report short. The goal is to decide what to work on next, not to produce a large document.

## Common measurement mistakes

- **Only checking one assistant.** Results can differ widely between assistants.
- **Including branded prompts.** They inflate your mention rate and hide real gaps.
- **Changing the prompt list constantly.** You lose the ability to compare over time.
- **Ignoring accuracy.** A wrong description counted as a mention makes your numbers look better than reality.
- **Reacting to a single run.** Wait for a pattern before changing your plans.

## Key takeaways

- AI share of voice measures how often assistants mention you, compared with competitors, for the questions your buyers ask.
- Use a fixed set of unbranded prompts and a fixed competitor list.
- Record mentions, position, accuracy, sentiment and citations for each assistant.
- Repeat checks and read trends rather than single answers.
- Compare the results with your Google rankings to find the most useful gaps.
