AI assistants choose which brands to mention by combining what their model learned during training with what they find when they search the web, then naming the brands that best fit the question and that several sources describe clearly and consistently. No AI company publishes an exact formula, and the systems change often, but the general mechanism is well understood and gives businesses practical levers to work with.

This guide walks through how an answer is put together, how the five main assistants differ, which signals are commonly observed to matter, and what you can do about each one.

## How an AI answer gets built

Although each product works differently, most assistant answers that mention brands follow a broadly similar path.

1. **Read the question.** The assistant works out what the user wants, including constraints such as location, budget, company size or a must-have feature.
2. **Decide whether to search.** For some questions it answers from what the model already knows. For others, especially those about current products, prices or local businesses, it may search the web.
3. **Gather information.** If it searches, it retrieves a set of pages and reads parts of them. If not, it relies on patterns learned from training data.
4. **Weigh and combine.** It pulls together the details that seem relevant and reliable, often favouring points that several sources agree on.
5. **Write the answer.** It produces a short response, frequently naming only a few brands, sometimes with links to sources.

Every step is a point where your brand can be included or dropped. A brand the model barely knows may be missed at step three. A brand with vague pages may lose out at step four.

## How the five main assistants differ

The following describes each product in broad, stable terms. Features change, so check each company's own documentation for current details.

| Assistant | Company | General approach relevant to brand mentions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | OpenAI | Answers from its model's knowledge and can search the web, showing links to sources when it does |
| Claude | Anthropic | Answers from its model's knowledge and can use web search where that feature is available, citing sources |
| Gemini | Google | Connected to Google's services, including Google Search, for up-to-date information |
| Grok | xAI | Integrated with X and can draw on posts on X as well as the wider web |
| Perplexity | Perplexity | Built around searching the web and citing sources alongside its answers |

Because the inputs differ, the same question can produce different brand lists in each assistant. That is why it is worth checking all five rather than assuming one represents the rest.

## Signals that commonly influence brand mentions

The points below are commonly observed patterns, not confirmed internals of any specific system. They follow from how language models and retrieval generally work.

### Relevance to the exact question

Assistants try to match the constraints in the prompt. A brand that clearly serves "small accounting firms in Canada" is a better fit for that question than a general tool that never mentions either. Specific pages about specific use cases help.

### Agreement across sources

When many independent sources describe a brand in similar terms, an assistant can repeat that description with more confidence. A brand mentioned on its own site only is harder to recommend than one that also appears in reviews, directories, comparison articles and industry coverage.

### Clarity and ease of extraction

Pages that state plainly what a product does, who it is for, what it costs and how it differs are easier to summarize. Facts hidden in images, long marketing copy or scripts that load late are harder for any system to use.

### Brand identity

If your name is shared with other companies or used inconsistently, the assistant may not connect information about you correctly. A clear about page, consistent naming and matching profiles across the web reduce confusion.

### Freshness

When an assistant searches, current pages can override older knowledge. When it answers from memory, it may repeat details from before its training cutoff. Keeping your site and listings up to date matters for the first case and limits the damage in the second.

### Reputation and sentiment

Reviews and discussions shape how a brand is described. Consistent complaints about the same issue may show up as a caution in the answer. Genuine positive experiences, described specifically, give the assistant concrete reasons to recommend you.

### Conversation context

Some assistants take into account earlier messages in the conversation and, in some products, saved preferences or the user's location. Two people asking the same question may see different brands for this reason.

## What you can influence

Most of these signals respond to ordinary, honest marketing work. Here is how each maps to an action.

| Signal | Action you can take |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance | Create pages for the specific use cases and customer types you serve |
| Agreement across sources | Keep listings accurate and earn genuine reviews and coverage |
| Clarity | State key facts in plain text near the top of relevant pages |
| Brand identity | Use one name and description consistently everywhere |
| Freshness | Update pages and profiles whenever products or prices change |
| Reputation | Respond to reviews and fix recurring problems customers raise |
| Crawler access | Allow the AI crawlers you want, based on a deliberate decision |

## A note on AI crawlers

AI companies run crawlers that read websites, and site owners can allow or block them in robots.txt. Commonly documented examples include GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot from OpenAI, ClaudeBot from Anthropic and PerplexityBot from Perplexity. Google offers a separate Google-Extended control that relates to use of content for its AI models, distinct from the Googlebot crawler used for search. Names and purposes can change, so confirm them in each company's documentation before editing your file.

Blocking a crawler may reduce what that assistant can read from your site. Some businesses block certain crawlers for content-licensing reasons, which is a legitimate choice. The important thing is to make the decision on purpose rather than by accident.

## A practical checklist

Work through this list for the questions that matter most to your business:

1. List the buyer questions you want to be mentioned for.
2. Ask each question in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity, in fresh conversations.
3. Record which brands are named, in what order, and which sources are cited.
4. Open the cited sources and check whether you appear and whether the information is correct.
5. Make sure your own site has a page that answers each question directly.
6. Fix inconsistent names, descriptions and categories across your listings.
7. Review your robots.txt against your decision on AI crawlers.
8. Repeat the checks on a regular schedule, since answers vary between runs.

## What does not work

- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating phrases does not make a brand more useful to recommend, and it makes pages harder to read.
- **Fake reviews or planted mentions.** They breach platform rules, can damage trust when discovered and do not create the consistent, credible picture assistants rely on.
- **Hidden text aimed at AI.** Instructions or text hidden from human visitors are a form of manipulation that can harm your reputation and your search standing.
- **Expecting instant results.** Models are retrained and indexes refresh on their own schedules. Improvements often show up gradually.

## Key takeaways

- Assistants combine training data and live web search, then name brands that fit the question and are described consistently.
- ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok and Perplexity use different inputs, so their recommendations differ.
- Commonly observed signals include relevance, agreement across sources, clarity, brand identity, freshness and reputation.
- Most levers are ordinary, honest work: clear pages, accurate listings, genuine reviews and deliberate crawler settings.
- Answers vary, so measure across repeated checks rather than one response.
